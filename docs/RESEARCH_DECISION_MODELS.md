# Decision models (CLM) and Token Superposition Training: what they could do for us

Written 2026-11-14. Reading and web research only; nothing here was run. Every statement is tagged
`(read: Sn)` for something read today at source Sn in the table below, or `(inference)` for my own
reasoning. Paper tables were extracted through an automated page reader, so spot-check a number before
quoting it elsewhere.

## Summary

- **CLM-8B is real, but it is a text ranker, not a computer-use model in our sense.** A frozen Qwen3-8B, the hidden state of its last token, and two small MLP heads (about 20M parameters, one 75.6 MB file, Apache-2.0) trained with InfoNCE; it scores candidate strings against a text state by cosine similarity (read: S1, S2, S4). No images, no coordinates, no generation.
- **"Jev" is TypeSafe's hosted decision model.** The "9x" is one benchmark (T-Rex: 16.5 against 149.8 ms), a local RTX 4090 against a hosted API. In the shipped result files CLM agrees with the planner's best move 66% of the time and Jev 99%; both survive all five runs with a safety shield on that replaces unsafe answers (4,883 interventions for CLM, 28 for Jev) (read: S3). The claim of comparable performance is not supported by the only result that can be inspected.
- **It does not fit the Mac.** As shipped it expects a vLLM embedding server and is set up for a 24 GB GPU, and the heads are locked to Qwen3-8B embeddings (read: S2, S4). It would be a second 8B model that cannot see the screen (inference). The multimodal CLM-35B promised for early October is not on the Hugging Face organisation today (read: S4).
- **The idea transfers; the model does not.** Pointing as selection is established: GUI-Actor and GUI-AIMA match or beat coordinate-writing models on the same backbones, most clearly on ScreenSpot-Pro, and GUI-Actor with a frozen backbone and a 19M to 103M head reaches 73.9 to 74.9 on ScreenSpot-v2 (read: S6, S7). Our own numbers agree: Bonsai kept choosing from an element list (43/50 against the parent's 46/50) and lost digit writing (read: S12).
- **A cell pick alone cannot close our gap.** One 32 px cell is 25 x 38 units on the 0-1000 scale, coarser than the tens digit (10 units) we are missing (inference from S12; GUI-Actor states the same limit, S6). It needs an in-cell offset or a zoomed second look.
- **A "System 1" model does not remove the 7 s.** Whatever looks at the screenshot must encode it; a selection head saves only the roughly ten answer tokens, about 0.35 s (inference). The part of CLM worth copying is caching the state separately from the question: encode the screenshot while the user is still speaking (inference, untested on our runtime).
- **TST does not apply.** Every reported run is pretraining from scratch; nothing on fine-tuning, existing checkpoints or quantised models (read: S11).

## Experiments, ranked

**1. Selection probe on frozen hidden states.** About one day; 1 to 2 GPU hours when the Spark is free; weights untouched. This is the smallest test of "pointing as selection".
- Step 0, minutes on a CPU: for every box in the select, report and Mac test sets, does it contain a cell centre at 1280 px? That share is the ceiling of a cell-centre answer and says how much the offset must carry. The ShowUI macOS median box is 176 x 66 px on screens 2880 or 3360 px wide, at most about 78 x 29 px at 1280, so roughly half the targets in that set are shorter than a cell (inference from S12).
- Head: query q is the state at the last prompt position, keys k_i are the states at the 1,040 image positions, score_i = (A q)·(B k_i) with A and B 4096 to 256 (2.1M parameters). Loss: KL to the cells overlapping the box, as in S6. Offset: a small MLP from [q, k_best] to (dx, dy) inside the cell. `Lab.hidden` needs a few lines to return image positions unpooled.
- Sweep the key layer over projector output, 8, 20 and final, and the query layer over 20 and final. Projector-output keys are the parent's own vision features, so that variant asks whether the ternary layers only have to say which element, not where. Run the same probe on the parent and UI-Venus as controls.
- Data: the four `mac:*:train` box sets (43,442 cases on 2,276 images, about 19 targets per image; cap `org_eclipse`) plus generated screens with functional wording, five questions per screenshot. Train online; caching states costs 8.5 MB per screen per layer.
- Measure hit rate on select plus report (318 screens, one standard error about 2.7 points) and on `mac:screen2ax_task:test` (2,106), paired against generated-digit answers from the same weights. Continue if the head wins by more than 6 points; stop if the best variant is at or below digits.
- What could go wrong: the cell is coarser than the tens digit; a frozen-backbone head has a ceiling (S6); the head cannot fix a wrong choice of element made in the damaged layers 4..19; Mac labels are short accessibility names, so the head may learn text matching; the head is tied to one set of weights and must be retrained after each change; on the Mac it needs hidden states out of the runtime, where only the final layer is likely to be exposed (inference, not checked).

**2. Zoomed second look, no training.** Half a day; forward passes only. Take the tuned Bonsai's first answer, crop a window of 192, 256 or 384 px (in the 1280 frame) from the original image, resize to 448 to 640 px (196 to 400 tokens), ask again, map back. Inside a 256 px window a tens-digit step is 2.6 px of screen instead of 12.8 px (inference). Report how often the window contains the target, hits after the second look, and answers lost and gained (S7 lost some). Cost on the Mac: 1.3 to 2.7 s more (inference from S12). The Mac screen has 2.3 times the linear resolution of the 1280 capture, so the crop adds real detail. Variant: a first look at 896 px (about 500 tokens, 3.4 s) plus the zoom may be both faster and more precise than one 7 s look; whether the hundreds digit holds at 896 px is unmeasured.

**3. Encode the screenshot while the user speaks.** Two to three hours, Mac only, no Spark needed. The lab builds the prompt with the image before the instruction (read: S12), so the 1,040 image tokens do not depend on the question. Send the image prefix at hotkey press with nothing to generate and prompt caching on; send the full prompt at release; measure time to first token against a cold request. Expected: the wait after speech falls from about 7 s to the instruction's 30 to 60 tokens (inference). Unknowns: whether PrismML's llama.cpp fork reuses the cache across requests for image chunks (try `mlx-vlm` if not), and the screen changing during speech (capture again at release, compare, re-encode only if different). Measure the vision encoder's time separately; the 7 s in `FINDINGS.md` is derived from the token rate.

**4. Selection loss as a training signal for the multipliers.** One to two days, only if experiment 1 shows signal. Add the head's cell loss as a second term in `TaskTuner`, multipliers of layers 4..19 open, head trained jointly, gentle restart. Hypothesis: a target over 1,040 cells tells the layers more about position than a ten-way digit choice, and may lift the digit read-out too; S7 trains a backbone this way. Score both read-outs and the canary. The model file stays an ordinary Bonsai; the head is a separate file of a few million parameters.

**5. Candidates from the accessibility tree, ranked as text.** About one day; optional. For Screen2AX screenshots the candidates are the tree's elements (role and label), the instruction is the Task command, the answer is the centre of the chosen box: exact, and no screenshot read. Compare (a) Bonsai choosing from a numbered list, the format where it scores 43/50 today, with (b) the CLM recipe on Bonsai's own last-token states: two small heads trained with InfoNCE on `mac:screen2ax_tree:train`. Limits: only a third of macOS apps expose a full tree (S10); a window with hundreds of elements is thousands of tokens unless candidate embeddings are cached per window, which is where CLM's caching would earn its place; this is a fast path for native apps, not a replacement for looking.

## Skip, and why

- **CLM-8B itself, and waiting for CLM-35B.** Text only, a second 8B model beside Bonsai, heads locked to another model's embeddings, quality claims that its own result files do not support, and no multimodal release to evaluate.
- **Jev.** A hosted API; its image locator is declared not ready for production by its maker (S5). Not a local option.
- **Set-of-marks with an external detector, or grid marks.** Adds a detector and a caption model, or extra passes; the evidence is for GPT-4o, and small models with grid marks score 22 to 33% on ScreenSpot-v2 (S8, S9), below what we have.
- **Reading location from raw attention without training.** 27 to 46% on ScreenSpot-v2 for Gemma-3 (S8). The trained probe in experiment 1 supersedes it and does not depend on which layers have attention maps.
- **TST.** It speeds up pretraining from scratch by spending more data to save compute, with a loss spike that takes thousands of steps to recover. Our runs are a few hundred steps on trained weights, and averaging neighbouring tokens would blur exactly the digit order we train (inference).

### 1. Open "Jev-style" decision models

- **None found outputs a click point.** They answer choice, yes/no and ordered-score questions. Only F3 has a box output (item 3).
- **Readout, three variants.** (a) JevStyle v1 and v2: the prompt lists lettered options and ends in an answer cue; the next-token logits of the option letters are renormalised; at most 26 options (read: F1). (b) JevStyle v3 and v4: each option's line ends in a slot token, and the option's score is the yes-minus-no logit at that slot, taken from the final normalised hidden state and the tied embedding rows; no parameters added (read: F1). (c) New heads: a scalar score head over each (state, question, option) triple (112 ms per question at 4 options, 560 ms at 77, so cost grows with options), or a joint schema head with a LoRA (read: F4).
- **Training.** v1: LoRA rank 16 on all linear layers, log-score loss, 22,000 examples, a fitted temperature folded into the last norm weight. v2: LoRA rank 32 (33.6M trainable), 60,000 records, 37 minutes on one H100. v3 and v4: full fine-tune; v4 keeps the vision tower frozen, averages two runs of about 2,245 steps, and uses soft labels distilled from Jev 1.14 (read: F1). Visual Jev: LoRA rank 16 on Qwen3-VL-4B, vision tower frozen, plain next-token cross-entropy at the readout position (read: F2).
- **Typed heads did not beat the model's own output layer.** Visual Jev built typed heads on the same interface and measured no accuracy gain at matched data, three seeds (read: F2).
- **Speed comes from one prefill pass, no sampling, and reading the state once.** v1: 77 ms per decision on an M1 Max, the same as the base model's single pass. v3: ten questions on a 4,000-token state run 4.6 times faster than one call per question. v4: 16 ms median on an A100 with recorded CUDA graphs, 72 ms without (read: F1). Visual Jev at 32 questions per image: 5.7 ms per question with a shared prefix, 19.3 ms batched without it, 50.7 ms serial; with one question the shared prefix is slower (82.9 against 48.1 ms); peak memory rises from 8.4 to 10.1 GiB (read: F2).
- **Images.** v4 takes images in the state at one token per 32 px square up to 1.6 megapixels, but image calls use its slower reference path and the MLX and GGUF builds are text only (read: F1).

### 2. TypeSafe's Jev documentation

- **How a Box is produced is not stated.** A Box answer is always four edges plus a confidence; there is no "not found" answer and no recommended threshold. For absence the page tells you to add a yes/no presence question to the same request, or a choice of none, one or several (read: F5).
- **Small targets.** The pages say a small target in a large image can be missed and suggest cropping first or asking again on a larger copy. Images must be 280 to 1,568 px per side and are rejected, not resized (read: F5).
- **Caching.** Within one request every question is evaluated against the same state and in isolation from the others; 13 questions in one call took 0.24 s against 2.81 s for 13 calls. Nothing is said about caching between requests, and each request must resend its images (read: F5). Whether the state is computed once internally is not stated.

### 3. Heads that output a point or box from hidden states

- **GUI-Actor's keys are not language-model states.** They are the vision encoder's patch features that feed the language model; only the query is a language-model state (the final layer at the action token). The head is one self-attention layer over the patch features plus two MLPs, about 20M (2B) and 100M (7B) parameters. The loss is next-token loss plus KL between the attention and a uniform mask over the patches the box touches (read: F6).
- **Its frozen-backbone result** (ScreenSpot-v2 73.9 and 74.9, ScreenSpot-Pro 25.4 and 22.9) comes with no explanation from the authors, and its data and length are not stated (read: F6). My reading: the query token was never trained to carry location, and one attention layer has to do all the matching between instruction and patches (inference).
- **Target shape matters more than head shape.** V2P keeps GUI-Actor's head and replaces the uniform mask with a Gaussian centred on the box, width set by the box size and integrated over each patch, plus a penalty on attention outside the box. ScreenSpot-Pro rises from 44.6 to 52.5; without the Gaussian it loses 5.0 points; small elements go from 17.5 to 23.8; it attributes 35.7% of the corrected errors to confusing an element's edge with its centre (read: F7). GUI-AIMA uses a similar centre-weighted target (read: S7).
- **Sub-patch precision in the literature is a second look, not an offset.** GUI-Actor (verifier on crops, weighted centre of neighbouring patches), GUI-AIMA (2x zoom, plus 7.7 on ScreenSpot-Pro), F3 (automatic crop pass when the first box covers under 10% of the image) and TypeSafe's own advice all crop and look again. V2P and GUI-Actor output a patch and stop. I found no GUI paper reporting offset regression or soft-argmax numbers.
- **Direct regression.** I found no GUI grounding paper that regresses (x, y) with a head and compares it with patch selection or digits (two searches). NExT-Chat decodes a location embedding with box and mask decoders instead of writing digits and reports gains on its own tasks, with no number against digit writing in the abstract. The RULER paper argues that the implicit mapping from patches to coordinates is the unstable part of digit writing and fixes it with explicit position reference tokens (read: F8, abstracts only).
- **One dense design worth copying.** F3's box head (4.9M parameters plus a crop-refinement head) reads five hidden-state layers at the image and question tokens, and decodes by grouping the image tokens whose boxes overlap the most likely box; so each image token proposes a box and the answer is a vote (read: F3; the per-token reading is my inference from the card's wording). It reports RefCOCO testB accuracy 85.3 at IoU 0.5.

### 4. A "click or not" output

- **GUI-Actor's verifier checks a point, not presence.** It is a separate 2B model shown the screenshot with a circle drawn at the candidate; it answers true or false. About 730,000 examples, half positive. Positives are box centres; negatives are the centre of another element in the same image or a random point outside the target box. It adds 3.5 points on ScreenSpot-Pro for the 7B and 8 to 9 points for the frozen-backbone models (read: F6, S6).
- **F3 separates presence from correctness.** Confidence is the probability the target is present times the probability the box is right given presence; presence and a count are returned too. Its negatives are absent categories of the same family, referring expressions that match nothing, and crops that differ in one attribute; presence AUROC 0.93 to 0.999, worst on the attribute crops; 1.5 to 13.8% of absent targets still get confidence above 0.5 (read: F3).
- **Others.** TypeSafe pairs a box with a presence question (F5). Visual Jev adds an explicit option meaning the question's premise is false (F2). The UI-Venus prompt our lab uses has an infeasible-task clause (read: S12).

### 5. Several suffixes from one cached prefix in transformers

- **Documented route:** fill a cache with the prefix under `torch.no_grad()`, then `copy.deepcopy(prompt_cache)` for each suffix and pass it as the cache; the example runs suffixes one at a time (read: F9, section on prefilling a cache). Caches are updated in place, hence the copy.
- **Hybrid models are covered by the cache classes.** The main branch has `LinearAttentionLayer` and `LinearAttentionAndFullAttentionLayer`, holding convolution and recurrent states beside keys and values (read: F9).
- **Batch expansion:** `Cache.batch_repeat_interleave(repeats)` exists, but the linear-attention layer classes override only `reorder_cache` and `crop` in the method list I read, so repeat-interleave may expand keys and values and leave the recurrent states at batch 1 (inference; bodies not read). `reorder_cache(beam_idx)` selects along the batch dimension for all state kinds, so `reorder_cache(torch.zeros(N, dtype=torch.long))` on a copy should expand batch 1 to N (inference). Check either route against a full forward pass before trusting it.
- **Rollback:** `crop` cannot roll back linear-attention layers unless `activate_past_recording()` was called before the forward pass (read: F9). Class names are from the main branch and may differ in the installed version.
- **Worked example** of shared-prefix batched suffixes on a Qwen vision model: `code/examples/quickstart.py` in F2 (no recurrent layers).

### Recommendation for the head

- **A fact about the keys.** With the image before the instruction and causal layers, the image-position states at every layer are independent of the instruction; that is why they can be cached. All matching between instruction and patches must happen in the head (inference). GUI-Actor's frozen result shows one dot product on a query the backbone was not trained to shape is the weak point.
- **Give the query side more to work with.** Read the instruction-token positions as well as the last prompt position (F3 reads question tokens), and let a small cross-attention block (one or two layers, a few heads) attend from them to the keys before the final score.
- **Keys from several layers.** Use a learned mix of projector output, a middle layer and layer 20, not one layer (F3 reads five). Projector output is the parent's own vision features and carries the finest detail; later layers add screen context. Layer 20 as the query layer is consistent with our swap results (parent layers 0..19 restore 0.725).
- **Selection loss.** KL to a Gaussian centred on the box centre with width proportional to the box, integrated over each cell, not a uniform box mask (F7). Add a penalty on score mass outside the box.
- **Point loss and sub-cell precision.** Soft-argmax only over the 3x3 or 5x5 cells around the best cell, then add the offset; a soft-argmax over all cells lands between two candidates (inference). Train the offset around the true cell so it learns even when selection is wrong. Scale the distance by the box size so small targets count. Also predict box width and height: it costs two numbers and tells the app when to take a zoomed second look.
- **Zoom only when needed.** Every published sub-patch gain comes from a second look. Trigger it when the predicted box is under about one cell or the confidence is low; it costs about 1.3 s on the Mac.
- **Click-or-not.** Keep it out of the softmax over cells, which cannot say "none": use a separate logit fed by the query, the peak score and the attended key, or a learned null cell. Train two labels if you can: target present, and own point inside the box (F3's product). Fit a temperature and a threshold on held-out Mac data; nobody publishes a threshold.
- **Negatives.** (a) An instruction whose label does not occur among this screenshot's accessibility labels (exact check on the Screen2AX and OS-Atlas sets). (b) Hard ones: a sibling of the same role or app that is absent here. (c) Crop the screenshot so the target falls outside, or re-render a generated screen without the element. Keep about half negative, as GUI-Actor's verifier did, and report presence AUROC and the false-click rate, since the hard attribute-level negatives were the weak spot in F3. ScreenSpot has no negatives, so this needs its own test set.
- **Baseline to beat for click-or-not.** The frozen model's own yes-minus-no logit at a slot (JevStyle's readout), with no new parameters.
- **Avoid:** a uniform box mask as the only target; global regression of (x, y) from the query alone (no supporting evidence found); a global soft-argmax; `batch_repeat_interleave` on the hybrid cache without a parity check; judging on 159 screens; training throughput plans that assume prefix sharing helps with one suffix per image (it was slower in F2).
